11 · End-to-End 7-Step Brokered OIDC Flow

The master walkthrough. Every request, redirect, cookie and header between partner_portal, ciam, csl and partner_portal_backend, in order, with what to look for in a HAR file at each step.

1. Zero-knowledge explanation

📘 Beginner Concept — the conference badge desk

You arrive at a conference (partner_portal) and ask to enter. They don't know you, so they send you to the registration desk (ciam). That desk doesn't check your ID itself: it sends you to the government ID office (csl), which checks your ID and a second proof (MFA). You walk back to the registration desk with a note; they issue a claim slip (authorization code) and send you back to the conference. The conference's back office (partner_portal_backend) privately hands the claim slip, plus the conference's own secret, to the registration desk and receives your badge (tokens), then checks the badge's hologram (signature).

  • Steps 1–5 are front channel: every message is a browser redirect (visible in the address bar and history).
  • Step 6 hands the code from the browser to the backend.
  • Step 7 is back channel: server-to-server TLS, secrets allowed.

2. High-level architecture

🏗️ Architecture Deep Dive — why two channels
The front channel is observable and tamperable by the user, so it carries only non-secret, short-lived values (state, code). Secrets (client_secret, code_verifier) and tokens only travel on the back channel between servers.

⚠️ Security Pitfall — where attacks land in this flow
  • Step 1/4: forged or reused state (login CSRF).
  • Step 5: code interception (no PKCE) or redirect_uri tampering.
  • Step 6: code replay by a second client.
  • Step 7: stolen client_secret; unvalidated iss/aud/nonce.
